Keel Over the Edge

Staying safe

Most people at sea are here to sail. This is how to keep it that way for you.

Who comes aboard

  • Let aboard only people you mean to sail with. If you don’t know who is knocking, choose Not now.
  • Close your boat to knocks when you would rather sail alone.
  • Put anyone ashore the moment you want them gone. You don’t have to say why.
  • Aboard someone else’s boat and not liking it? Go ashore: your own boat goes back in the water.

Mute, block and report

Press and hold someone’s name or one of their lines in the chat (on a computer, right-click it), or use the keys beside their name in Crew.

  • Mute: you stop seeing their lines. They are not told.
  • Block: neither of you sees the other’s lines, and they can’t knock on your boat. If you are the skipper, they go ashore. They are not told.
  • Report: tell the game’s owner what is wrong. What was said aboard lately goes with your report for the owner to read. Nobody is ever told who reported them.
  • Report at once. Chat is never kept: a line is gone after 30 minutes, and after that there is nothing left to show the owner.
  • Your mutes and blocks are listed on the account page, where you can end any of them.

Keep your life ashore to yourself

  • Never share contact details: your phone number, where you live or go to school, your email, or your name on other apps or games. Nobody in the game needs them.
  • Never move to another app to talk to someone you met here, however friendly they seem.
  • Never give anyone your recovery code: it signs your sailor in.

If something feels wrong

  • Leave: put them ashore, go ashore, or close the game.
  • Report them, then block them.
  • Tell someone you trust: a parent, a friend, a teacher.

Telling the owner from outside the game

Anyone can tell the game’s owner about something that happened in the game, with a sailor or without one: a parent or carer, a teacher, anyone. Use the notice page.

Or write to the owner at [the owner’s contact address].

Where to get help

  • If anyone is in danger now, call your local emergency number: 111 in New Zealand, 000 in Australia, 999 in the United Kingdom, 911 in the United States and Canada, 112 in much of the rest of the world.
  • If a crime may have been committed, tell the police where you live. They can act where the game cannot.
  • New Zealand: Netsafe, free help with anything that goes wrong online, netsafe.org.nz.
  • Australia: the eSafety Commissioner, who helps with online abuse and can have harmful content removed, esafety.gov.au.
  • A child exploited or put at risk online, anywhere: NCMEC’s CyberTipline, report.cybertip.org.
