Staying safe

Most people at sea are here to sail. This is how to keep it that way for you.

Who comes aboard

  • Let aboard only people you mean to sail with. If you don’t know who is knocking, choose Not now.
  • Close your boat to knocks when you would rather sail alone.
  • Put anyone ashore the moment you want them gone. You don’t have to say why.
  • Aboard someone else’s boat and not liking it? Go ashore: your own boat goes back in the water.

Keep your life ashore to yourself

  • Never share contact details: your phone number, where you live or go to school, your email, or your name on other apps or games. Nobody in the game needs them.
  • Never move to another app to talk to someone you met here, however friendly they seem.
  • Never give anyone your recovery code: it signs your sailor in.

If something feels wrong

  • Leave: put them ashore, go ashore, or close the game.
  • Tell someone you trust: a parent, a friend, a teacher.

Where to get help

  • If anyone is in danger now, call the police or emergency services: 111 in New Zealand, 000 in Australia, 999 in the United Kingdom, 911 in the United States and Canada, 112 in much of the rest of the world.
  • New Zealand: Netsafe, free help with anything that goes wrong online, netsafe.org.nz.
  • Australia: eSafety, which helps with online abuse and can have harmful content removed, esafety.gov.au.
  • A child exploited or put at risk online, anywhere: NCMEC’s CyberTipline, report.cybertip.org.
