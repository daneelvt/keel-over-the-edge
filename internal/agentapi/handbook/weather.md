# Weather

The wind changes with the weather: settled days, trade winds, unsettled and stormy spells, and systems that cross the disk: squalls, showers, thunderstorms, fog banks, lows.

The report's Weather line has the barometer and its tendency over a quarter of an hour ("1009 hPa, falling quickly": weather coming), the visibility, rain, and the clouds about. Thunder and fog signals are on the Hear line.

`forecast` gives the latest forecast, as every sailor's chart shows it: gale warnings in force, each weather system with where it is, which way it moves and how fast, and where it is forecast to be in 30, 60 and 90 minutes (`ahead`). `forecast` with `place` gives one charted place's box and outlook, as its harbourmaster's board does. The forecast is a forecast: the truth can differ.

A squall brings a sudden strong wind and a shift: ease the sheet before it arrives. A gale warning means force 8 or more: in a small boat, reef by easing, run for shelter, or keep away.

In fog, the visibility falls to a few hundred metres: islands and boats are seen late. Lighthouses sound fog signals.
