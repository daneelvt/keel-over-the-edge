# Sailing

The wind's direction is where it comes from. "42° off the bow, port side": you are on port tack, 42° off the wind.

Points of sail, by angle off the true wind:

- under 30°: in irons. The sail flogs and the boat stops.
- about 40°: close-hauled. To go upwind, sail close-hauled and tack from side to side.
- 90°: a beam reach, fastest in a breeze.
- 180°: dead downwind. A gybe turns your stern through the wind.

In 6 knots of wind: 3.3 knots at 40° (2.4 made good to windward), 4.5 at 90°, 3.1 at 150°, 2.8 at 170° (2.7 made good downwind). Downwind in light air, run at 170°: it beats reaching and gybing. At 90°: 7.3 knots in 12, 9.6 in 20.

Gusts are darker patches on the water; the Gust line says when one arrives. Ease the sheet before it hits, or the boat heels too far and capsizes: about 15 seconds in the water. "In the wind shadow of B2" means that boat's sail takes your wind.

## Light air

Rudder force goes with speed squared: under half a knot through the water the Boat line says "no steerage", and a turn may stall. Sail a reach, boom out half the apparent wind's angle, and gather way before turning; to change side, bear away and gybe. Or wait: `forecast` says when wind comes. "In the lee of land" on the Wind line means more wind further out.

## Shoal water and running aground

The board reaches 0.8 m down. The Shoal line gives water under 1.5 m by bearing and range, and "clear" the bearings free of it to 300 m: off a bank, steer for the middle of a clear span. Aground and still for 5 s, you are pushed off into 2 m of water the way you came, at rest. Going astern the rudder works in reverse: the Boat line says "astern". By hand, put the helm the other way.
