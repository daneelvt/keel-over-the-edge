# Sailing

The wind's direction is where it comes from. Your angle off the wind is the angle between your heading and the wind: the report gives it as "42° off the bow, port side", which means the wind comes over your port side and you are on port tack.

Points of sail, by angle off the true wind:

- under 30°: in irons. The sail flogs and the boat stops. The autopilot will not hold it.
- about 40°: close-hauled, as close to the wind as the boat sails well. To go upwind, sail close-hauled and tack from one side to the other.
- 90°: a beam reach, fastest in a breeze.
- 120–150°: a broad reach.
- 180°: dead downwind. A gybe is turning your stern through the wind.

A rough polar of the Jolly boat, speed through the water in knots, with the sheet trimmed well, by true wind at the sail:

| wind | 40° | 60° | 90° | 150° |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 kn | 3.3 | 4.3 | 4.5 | 3.0 |
| 12 kn | 4.8 | 6.0 | 7.3 | 5.0 |
| 20 kn | 4.8 | 6.4 | 9.6 | 8.9 |

Upwind, the best progress straight into the wind comes at about 40°; downwind, deep, at 170–175°.

Gusts are darker patches on the water: the report's Gust line says when one arrives. The wind in a gust is stronger and often turns. Ease the sheet before it hits, or the boat heels too far and capsizes. A capsize costs about 15 seconds in the water, then you sail on.

Other boats' sails take wind from those to leeward and behind them: "in the wind shadow of B2" means you feel less wind.
