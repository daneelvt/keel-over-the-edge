# Contributor agreement: your signature and your privacy

When you sign the Keel Over the Edge Contributor Agreement, a record of your
signature is kept. This notice says what is kept, why, and what you can do
about it.

**Who keeps it.** Daneel van Tonder, the owner of Keel Over the Edge, in New
Zealand, who is also the privacy officer for it. You can reach them at the
address on the signing page, https://play.keelovertheedge.com/agreement.

**What is kept.** Your GitHub account's number and its name when you signed,
the full name you give, the date and time, the agreement's version and the
SHA-256 digest of its text. If you are under 18, also your parent's or
guardian's name, the date of their consent, and their e-mail. Nothing else:
no address, country, phone number or e-mail of yours. When you sign in with
GitHub, the signing page asks GitHub only who you are, and keeps no access to
your account.

Also kept, while it waits for a signature, is which of your open pull
requests is waiting, so that it can be checked again the moment you sign.

**Why.** To show that the game's owner may use and license your
contributions, as the agreement says, for as long as anyone could ask. For
signers in the European Union or the United Kingdom, the basis under the GDPR
is the owner's legitimate interest in being able to prove that licence. It is
not consent, because a licence that could be withdrawn would not do its job.

**Who sees it.** The owner alone. Nothing about a signer is published: not on
the game's site, not in the repository, not in a pull request. A pull
request's check says only whether everyone who wrote it has signed.

**Where.** In the game's database, on its server in the United States, and in
its encrypted backups at Backblaze, also in the United States. Both hold it
for the owner and use it for nothing else. No other service holds it.

**For how long.** As long as your contribution is part of the game the owner
distributes, and six years after: the time within which a claim about it
could be brought in New Zealand.

**Seeing it and correcting it.** You can see your signatures on the signing
page at any time, and download each with the text you signed. To ask for a
correction, or for anything the owner holds about you, write to the address on
the signing page. You may also complain to New Zealand's Privacy
Commissioner, or to the data protection authority where you live.

**Deleting it.** A signature cannot be deleted while it is needed to show the
owner's right to use your contribution (in New Zealand, information privacy
principle 9; under the GDPR, Article 17(3)(e)). A signature made by mistake is
marked void, with the reason, and is no longer used.
