The game connection's framing, and the snapshot's layout.

Framing
-------

One WebSocket binary message is one game message. Its first byte is its
kind:

  1  a Protocol Buffers message: ClientMessage from the client,
     ServerMessage from the server (keel/v1/game.proto)
  2  the snapshot below, from the server

Any other kind, and any text message, closes the connection with 1003.

The snapshot
------------

The player's own boat in full, then the other boats in the player's view
as changes against a base: a snapshot the connection sent before, which
the client must still hold. After the kind byte, little-endian, a header of
175 bytes (176 with the kind byte); then, while the boat's autopilot is in
use, its block of 56 bytes; then, while the boat is aground or over shoal water, its ground
block of 24 bytes; then, while its headsails, yards, reef, downwind sail or
its sailor's place aft are not zero, its rig's block of 104 bytes; then, while the boat is falling over the lip,
its over block of 8 bytes; then the entries:

  offset  bytes  field
       0      1  layout: 16
       1      8  tick (int64)
       9      2  base: how many ticks before this one the base snapshot is
                 (uint16); 0 for none: the view starts empty
      11      1  flags: bit 0, the far band is sampled by this snapshot;
                 bit 1, the autopilot's block follows the header; bit 2,
                 the ground block follows it, or the header; bit 3, the
                 over block follows the blocks before it, or the header;
                 bit 4, the boat stays where it is, not stepped, while its
                 owner's free camera roams; bit 5, the rig's block follows
                 the blocks before it, or the header; bit 6, the
                 connection does not hold the boat's controls: its inputs
                 steer nothing, and it predicts nothing (a sailor holds
                 their own boat's unless they handed them to someone
                 aboard, a crew member once handed them, a spectator
                 never); the rest 0
      12      4  seq of the own boat's control word in force (uint32)
      16      2  arrival margin in ticks (int16): the lowest since the
                 previous snapshot, how many ticks before its tick an input
                 arrived (negative: after); -32768 for none
      18      2  helm index in force (uint16), 0 … 1024
      20      2  sheet index in force (uint16), 0 … 1024
      22    152  the own boat's state, 19 float64s in this order:
                 x, y, heading, surge, sway, yawRate, heel, rollRate,
                 boom, boomRate, sailor, rudder, sheetLimit, sailorMode,
                 sailorTimer, heave, heaveRate, pitch, pitchRate
     174      1  entries that follow, 0 … 128
     175      …  the autopilot's block, if flag bit 1; then the ground
                 block, if flag bit 2; then the rig's block, if flag bit 5;
                 then the over block, if flag bit 3; then the entries

The autopilot's block, sent while the pilot word in force or what the
autopilot remembers is not zero (-0 counts), so a boat that never uses it
pays nothing:

     175      1  mode in force: 0 off, 1 an angle off the wind, 2 a heading
     176      1  way of the latest order: 0 hold, 1 tack, 2 gybe
     177      1  order count, 0 … 3
     178      1  0
     179      2  target (uint16), 65536ths of a turn
     181      2  0
     183     48  what the autopilot remembers, 6 float64s: pilotTrim,
                 pilotWay, pilotOrder, pilotWatch, pilotMark, pilotLeft;
                 the state's fields after the boat's, 0 when the block is
                 absent. pilotWay is also 3 or 4 here: a tack or a gybe
                 that stalled
     231      …  the ground block, if flag bit 2; else the entries

The ground block, sent while the time the boat has been aground and all
but still, or where it left deep water, is not zero (-0 counts), so a boat
in deep water pays nothing; at the offset where the blocks before it end:

       0     24  3 float64s: groundTimer, deepX, deepY; the state's
                 fields after the autopilot's, 0 when the block is absent
      24      …  the rig's block, if flag bit 5; else the over block, if
                 flag bit 3; else the entries

The rig's block, sent while the reef in force or any of the state's rig
fields is not zero (-0 counts), so a boat with no headsails, yards or
downwind sail under full sail, whose rig does not pitch her, pays nothing;
at the offset where the blocks before it end:

       0      1  the reef in force (the rig word's): 0 full sail, 1 or 2
       1      7  0
       8     96  12 float64s: jib, jibRate, jibLimit, yards, foreYards,
                 reef, hoist, hoistTarget, kite, kiteRate, kiteLimit,
                 sailorAft; the state's last twelve fields, 0 when the
                 block is absent
     104      …  the over block, if flag bit 3; else the entries

The over block, sent while the boat has gone over the lip and is falling,
before it starts again at the start: the boat is not stepped while it
falls, and its state is as it went over. At the offset where the blocks
before it end:

       0      8  the tick the boat went over the lip (int64), above 0 and
                 at most the snapshot's tick
       8      …  the entries

A boat that stays is held where it is, as one falling over the lip is,
until a snapshot without bit 4 lets it go.

A layout byte other than 16 is refused, as are a mode, way, order or reef
out of range, a spare byte not 0 and an over block's tick out of its range. The wind is not in the snapshot: the client
computes the wind at its boat from the spells of weather it is sent
(WeatherSystems), as the server does.

The view
--------

The view is up to 64 other boats, each in a view slot, 0 … 63, of the
connection's own numbering. Another boat's heave and pitch are not sent:
each client floats it on the waves it draws under it, which are the same
waves for everyone, and adds the trim of a boat whose rig pitches her. A
view slot's boat is:

  kind       an index into the catalog's boats
  look       the sailor's look code: five fields of 4 bits from the
             low end, each a place in a list of the catalog's: the
             look among its sailors (15 for the machine), the
             version, the skin (a machine's finish), the hair (0 for
             a machine) and the main colour; at most 20 bits
  boat look  the boat's own look code: its hull's paint in bits 0–3
             and its sails' cloth in bits 4–7, places in the
             catalog's boatLooks lists, and its sail number in bits
             8–21; at most 22 bits. Its name, player text, comes
             apart, in BoatNames
  flags      bit 0: in the far band; bits 1–2: the sailor's mode
             (sailing, in the water, on the board, climbing); bit 3: the
             boat has gone over the lip and is falling, not stepped; bit
             4: the boat is a machine sailor's, sailed by an AI agent,
             and carries the machine mark on its sail; bit 5, in an
             enter, the boat is the one the slot already holds, its
             sailor's look, its own look or its kind changed (swapped
             in port); bits 6–7, the reef the sail is at,
             rounded to a level: 0 full sail, 1 or 2
  x, y       position, cm east and north of the world's centre, each
             within a signed 24-bit integer
  heading    uint16, 2π/65536 rad a step, clockwise from north
  heel       int16, 2π/65536 rad a step, positive starboard down
  boom       int16, 2π/65536 rad a step, positive to starboard
  rudder     int16, 2π/65536 rad a step, positive turning the bow to
             starboard
  sailor     int16, the sailor's offset in cm, positive to starboard
  sail       the sail byte: the sailor's flattening in bits 0–3 (0 … 15
             for 0 … 1), the foot strip's flow in bits 4–5 and the head
             strip's in bits 6–7 (0 luffing, 1 attached, 2 stalled,
             3 aback)
  jib        int16, 2π/65536 rad a step: the headsails' clew's angle off
             the centreline, positive to starboard; 0 with no headsails
  yards      int16, 2π/65536 rad a step: the after yards' angle off the
             centreline, signed by the side they are braced for; 0 with
             no square sails
  fore       int16, 2π/65536 rad a step: the fore yards' angle less the
             after yards', while the crew hauls them round apart in a
             turn; else 0
  kite       int16, 2π/65536 rad a step: the downwind sail's clew's angle
             off the centreline, positive to starboard, while it is
             hoisted at all; else 0, and 0 with no downwind sail
  hoist      uint8: how far the downwind sail is hoisted, 0 in its bag
             … 255 set; 0 with none
  trim       int8, π/360 rad a step: the hull's pitch off the water's
             surface, positive with the bow up, on a boat whose rig
             pitches her; else 0
  aft        int8, cm: how far aft of their place the sailor stands, on
             a boat whose rig pitches her; else 0

Angles are wrapped into [-π, π) before they are stepped.

The entries
-----------

Each entry begins with one byte: the view slot in bits 0–5 and the op in
bits 6–7. Applied in order to the base's view:

  op 0  update   a change mask, one byte: bit 0 position, 1 heading,
                 2 heel, 3 boom, 4 rudder, 5 sailor, 6 sail, 7 flags; then
                 the fields set, in that order: the position as two zigzag
                 varints, the change in cm of x and of y since the base;
                 the others as an enter has them. The slot must hold a
                 boat, and the mask must not be 0.
  op 1  enter    a boat in full: kind and rig (uvarint: the kind × 64,
                 plus 1 if jib follows, 2 if yards, 4 if fore, 8 if kite,
                 16 if hoist, 32 if trim and aft), look
                 (uvarint), boat look (uvarint), flags (1), x and y (int24 each), heading,
                 heel, boom, rudder, sailor (2 each), sail (1); then
                 jib, yards, fore and kite (2 each), hoist (1), and trim
                 and aft (1 each), those the rig names: those not 0 (trim
                 and aft not both 0), which are 0 when not sent. An enter on a slot that
                 holds a boat replaces it. A sailor's look, the boat's
                 look or its kind changed is sent as an enter, whatever
                 the band; an update never changes them.
  op 2  leave    nothing more: the slot's boat leaves the view. The slot
                 must hold a boat.
  op 3  update   an update with a change mask of two bytes, the first as
        of the   op 0's, the second for the rig: bit 0 jib, 1 yards,
        rig      2 fore, 3 kite, 4 hoist, 5 trim, 6 aft; then the fields
                 set, the first byte's in its order, then the second's.
                 The second byte must not be 0, nor have bit 7 set: an update of the first byte's
                 fields alone is op 0's, so a boat with no rig costs not
                 a byte more than it did.

Refused: varints written longer than they need be, a position outside 24
bits, flags with their spare bits set or a reef of 3, a kind above 65535,
a look of more than 20 bits, a boat look of more than 22, an enter's
rig field sent as 0, and bytes after the last entry.

Samples
-------

Every boat in the view after a snapshot that is in the near band, or has
an entry in it, is sampled by it: its values are the boat's at the
snapshot's tick. A boat in the far band without an entry is sampled only
when the header's bit 0 is set. A sampled boat without an entry has not
changed since the base.
